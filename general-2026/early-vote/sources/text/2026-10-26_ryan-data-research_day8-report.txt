                                           Notes and Definitions
Registered Voter Total: The total listed on page one is based on the list of registered voters Ryan Data & Research
received from the Texas Secretary of State on earlier this month. The number of current registered voters may be lower
than the number of registered voters reported by the Texas Secretary of State in November 2026.
Previous Election History: Who votes in a specific election is considered public information. Following each election,
Ryan Data & Research requests a list of everyone who voted and appends this information to a list of all registered voters.
Which elections an individual votes in is considered public information. Who a person votes for on the ballot is
NOT public.
When analyzing voters based on their previous election history, Ryan Data & Research takes into consideration each
voter's election history over the previous four election cycles and then classifies each person into the following categories:
   Rep (No D): Voters who have voted in a previous Republican Primary and have no previous Democratic Primary
   history.
   Mixed Pri / R: Voters who have previously voted in both party's primary, but most recently voted in a Republican
   Primary. For example, if an individual voted in the 2022 Republican Primary but voted in the 2020 Democratic Primary,
   that individual would be included in this category.
   Dem (No R): Voters who have voted in a previous Democratic Primary and have no previous Republican Primary
   history.
   Mixed Pri / D: Voters who have previously voted in both party's primary, but most recently voted in a Democratic
   Primary. For example, if an individual voted in the 2022 Democratic Primary but voted in the 2020 Republican Primary,
   that individual would be included in this category.
   Gen Only: Voters who have not previously voted in a primary (R or D) but have previous General Election history.
   No History: Voters who have not previously voted in a primary (R or D) and have no previous General Election history.
   This group includes people who recently turned 18 and are voting for the first time, people who have recently moved to
   Texas and are voting in Texas for the first time, but it also may include people who have been registered to vote in the
   state for and have simply been a dormant voter over the past few elections.
Please note, voting in a party’s primary does not mean the individual voted for that party’s candidates in a general election.
In some cases, Democrats vote in the Republican Primary and Republicans vote in the Democratic Primary. However,
typically, 95%+ of voters participating in a party primary end up voting for that party’s candidates in the general election.

Gender: The Texas Secretary of State maintains information on the gender of all registered voters. However, there are
some records that don’t include that information. The most likely reason for this is that the individual failed to include that
information on their voter registration card when it was submitted to the voter registrar. Voters who are not classified as
male or female are therefore placed into the “unknown” category.
Republican Primary Voters: As mentioned earlier, each voter’s election history over the previous four election cycles is
analyzed. If an individual participates in a Republican Primary, they are placed into one of the four categories:
1Rs: The individual has voted in exactly one of the last four Republican Primaries.
2Rs: The individual has voted in exactly two of the last four Republican Primaries.
3Rs: The individual has voted in exactly three of the last four Republican Primaries.
4Rs: The individual has voted in all four of the last four Republican Primaries.

Democratic Primary Voters: As mentioned earlier, each voter’s election history over the previous four election cycles is
analyzed. If an individual participates in a Democratic Primary, they are placed into one of the four categories:
1Ds: The individual has voted in exactly one of the last four Democratic Primaries.
2Ds: The individual has voted in exactly two of the last four Democratic Primaries.
3Ds: The individual has voted in exactly three of the last four Democratic Primaries.
4Ds: The individual has voted in all four of the last four Democratic Primaries.

Discrepancies Between Numbers: This report includes information from several different sources. Most information
included in this report is based on data from the voter registration rolls. However, the report also includes information from
the official numbers, as reported by the Texas Secretary of State and from the Texas Legislative Council. There may be
differences between these numbers for a variety of reasons. The list of registered voters is constantly changing.
Information for previous elections is based on the voter file Ryan Data & Research used during that election cycle. That
number may differ from the official total reported by the Texas Secretary of State. Redistricting also could be a reason for a
discrepancy in numbers. Ryan Data & Research used best efforts to take voter information from the 2016-2020 election
cycles and assign it to the current district. In some cases, assigning new district information to data from the previous
election cycles was not possible.

R Primary Vote Advantage: This is the total number of voters who had previous Republican Primary election history
minus the total number of voters who had previous Democratic Primary election history. As a group, voters without any
previous primary election history tend to be closer to the center and/or to the left on the political spectrum. A larger margin
between Republican Primary voters and Democratic Primary voters typically means that a Republican is more likely to win,
even if non-primary voters skew for the Democrat.

Participation Rates: This calculation is based on the number of voters within a specified group and how many from the
group actually voted. For example, if there are 100 individuals with previous Republican Primary history and 75 vote in a
specific election, that group has a 75% participation rate.

Voting Method: The Texas Secretary of State identifies how each person casts their vote. In Texas, there are three
methods for voting: voting by mail, voting early in person, and voting on Election Day. The voting by mail category includes
only those individuals who submitted their completed ballot. If an individual requested a ballot and did not return it, they
are not considered to have voted in the election.
Voters by Day: The date a person voted is based on the rosters provided by the counties to the Secretary of State.
Individuals who voted by mail are included in the total based on the day their completed ballot was received AND
processed by the county clerk. If a voter submitted their completed ballot prior to the beginning of early voting, they are
included in the day one total. It is possible that some day's voters were reported on a different day. For example, if a
county did not submit a daily roster of early voters for day two and included those voters on the list they submitted on day
three, it is possible that all the voters from day two are flagged as having voted on day three and not on day two.
